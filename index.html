Approach

The core of my approach is targeted and specific. I work to identify core weaknesses and strengths in a student's skillset, ensuring that I praise strengths and find practical solutions to work on their weaknesses. My focus is to fill in the gaps that school teachers generally leave behind — in a hands-on and practical way.

One important part of my approach is that I build skills alongside content. I don't believe speaking at children for an hour is very helpful — the goal of my lessons is for my students to be active as opposed to passive. I teach through a series of exercises that encourage independence of thought: simple, chunked, fun puzzles that help the practical application of the subject. I also encourage web-based learning, for example using gamification to memorise large chunks of information and developing quizzes to aid vocabulary learning.

In essence, my tutoring is about breaking down skills into discrete chunks, and then working on those weaknesses without discouraging the pupil. Despite this rigour of preparation, I have a very relaxed, calming attitude during class. There is a large body of scientific research indicating that improving at any skill requires two things: (a) focused, discrete improvement on targeted weaknesses and (b) honest, clear feedback from an external source. My tutoring is informed by both principles.

My goal is not to instruct — it is to give students skills and core techniques so that they no longer need me as a tutor. I see my lessons much like a sports coaching session: a tennis coach would never spend an hour explaining how to hit a forehand — they would get their students doing personalised exercises to keep things engaging. My main objective is ultimately to be no longer necessary.

My Approach

How I Teach

The core of my approach is targeted and specific. I identify core weaknesses and strengths in a student's skillset — praising what they do well and finding practical solutions for what they don't. My focus is to fill the gaps that school teachers leave behind, in a hands-on and practical way.

I build skills alongside content. I don't believe speaking at children for an hour is helpful — my lessons are designed so students are active, not passive. I use discrete, chunked exercises: simple, specific puzzles that encourage independence of thought and make learning genuinely engaging.

My tutoring is informed by two well-evidenced principles: (a) focused, discrete improvement on targeted weaknesses, and (b) honest, clear feedback from an external source. I see each lesson like a sports coaching session — a tennis coach would never spend an hour explaining how to hit a forehand; they would get their student doing targeted exercises. My goal is ultimately to make myself unnecessary: to give students the skills they need to succeed independently.

In English I focus on building a core set of transferable skills: comprehension, writing, vocabulary, creative writing, grammar and punctuation.

Comprehension: I focus on identifying question types and practicing specific reading strategies. My focus is on ensuring students understand what is being asked of them, before working on getting the best marks possible by understanding mark schemes and being strategic.

Writing: I believe the way to improve writing is through a series of small, discrete writing exercises. I teach children to plan their ideas visually and focus on a series of small craft techniques that will improve their writing: for example, developing detailed and specific description, improving sentence openings, varying sentence length, and using show-not-tell.

Vocabulary: I develop and track vocabulary lists and encourage web-based learning, for example using Quizlet and developing quizzes to aid vocabulary learning.

In Maths I identify and map out core areas of weakness in a student's understanding and address each one systematically, from the ground up, using targeted exercises.

I use a variety of methods to explain each topic — bean counters, a deck of cards, real-life problems — before prioritising retention. I am a firm believer in spaced repetition: consistently threading previous topics back into each session so that knowledge is properly ingrained over time. Alongside this, I use gamification — for example, using Prodigy, Khan Academy or Times Table Rockstars — to make learning fun and to track progress.

For all students, I focus on building core Maths skills — times tables, short and long division, place value — before working on specific areas of weakness. Even for students who are performing well, it is often possible to identify small gaps in core skills that are causing problems downstream. By addressing these core skills, we can unlock progress across the board.

One of the most important things for me as a tutor is that I leave a student better than I found them. I aim to build an atmosphere of communal activity — using "we" throughout, because I believe the tutor and the student are a team with a shared goal.

Despite the rigour of preparation, I maintain a relaxed, encouraging atmosphere in class. I do not lecture or scold. My aim is for every student to leave each session feeling more capable and more confident than when they arrived. For students who are feeling discouraged, I make sure to give encouragement and celebrate small wins.

I also focus on developing a growth mindset in students — fostering a belief that intelligence is not fixed, and that with effort and the right strategies, anyone can improve. I am particularly mindful of students who have come to believe they are "not a Maths person" or "not a reader" — often this is the result of a specific and identifiable gap in their knowledge that, once filled, unlocks confidence and enthusiasm for the subject.
