Approach

From the first lesson, I try to always establish what the goals of our sessions are, so there is an immediate concrete goal (exam, coursework, etc). I try to lay out as clearly as possible what the process of achieving that goal looks like.

I try to build an atmosphere of communal activity, treating all children as equals. I generally use "we" when speaking, because I believe the tutor and the child are a team, with a common, shared goal.

One important part of my approach is that I build skills alongside content. I don't believe speaking at children for an hour is very helpful — the goal of my lessons is for my students to be active as opposed to passive.

I teach these skills throughout our lessons through a series of exercises that encourage independence of thought. Discrete exercises are the core of my teaching practice — simple, chunked, fun puzzles that help the practical application of the subject.

I also encourage web-based learning, for example using gamification to memorise large chunks of information and developing quizzes to aid vocabulary learning.

In essence, my tutoring is about breaking down skills into discrete chunks, and then working on those weaknesses without discouraging the pupil. Despite this rigour of preparation, I have a very relaxed, calming attitude during class.

There is a large body of scientific research indicating that improving at any skill requires two things: (a) focused, discrete improvement on targeted weaknesses and (b) honest, clear feedback from an external source. My tutoring is informed by these two principles — and through hard work I genuinely believe I can teach children the skills to succeed not just in their exams but in later life.

English

In English I focus on building a core set of transferable skills: comprehension, writing, vocabulary, creative writing, grammar and punctuation.

Comprehension

I focus on identifying question types and practising specific reading strategies. My focus is on ensuring students understand what is being asked of them, before working on getting the best marks possible by understanding mark schemes and being strategic.

Writing

I believe the way to improve writing is through a series of small, discrete writing exercises. I teach children to plan their ideas visually and focus on craft techniques: for example, developing detailed and specific description, improving sentence openings, varying sentence length, and using show-not-tell.

Vocabulary

I develop and track vocabulary lists and encourage web-based learning — for example using Quizlet and developing quizzes to aid vocabulary retention.

Maths

In Maths I identify and map out core areas of weakness in a student's understanding and address each one systematically, from the ground up, using targeted exercises.

I use a variety of methods to explain each topic — bean counters, a deck of cards, real-life problems — before prioritising retention. I am a firm believer in spaced repetition: consistently threading previous topics back into each session so that knowledge is properly ingrained over time. Alongside this, I use gamification — for example Prodigy, Khan Academy or Times Table Rockstars — to make learning engaging and to track progress.

For all students, I focus on building core Maths skills — times tables, short and long division, place value — before working on specific areas of weakness. Even for students who are performing well, it is often possible to identify small gaps in core skills that are causing problems downstream.

Pastoral

One of the most important things for me as a tutor is that I leave a student better than I found them. I aim to build an atmosphere of communal activity — using "we" throughout, because the tutor and the student are a team with a shared goal.

Despite the rigour of preparation, I maintain a relaxed, encouraging atmosphere in class. My aim is for every student to leave each session feeling more capable and more confident than when they arrived. For students who are feeling discouraged, I make sure to give encouragement and celebrate small wins.

I also focus on developing a growth mindset in students — fostering a belief that intelligence is not fixed, and that with effort and the right strategies, anyone can improve. I am particularly mindful of students who have come to believe they are "not a Maths person" or "not a reader" — often this is the result of a specific and identifiable gap in their knowledge that, once filled, unlocks confidence and enthusiasm for the subject.
